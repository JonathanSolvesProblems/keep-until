# Keep Until charter

The metaphor: the canning line's date coder, run in reverse. A can arrives with a stamp nobody
can read; Keep Until reads it and prints it again, large and clear, with what to do.

1. **Dates and the name are printed in the coder's dots, in upper case.** Every date the app
   reads or computes (the stamp, the keep-until date) and the name KEEP UNTIL are set in Doto,
   capitals only, the way a coder prints. No other words are. Because the dot matrix is the
   stamp on the can; the name is the one thing the coder prints that is not evidence.
2. **Words are set in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.** Because volunteers are any age, often on
   their first shift, reading from about a metre away; this face was drawn for low-vision readers.
3. **The verdict is the largest thing on the screen, and the stamp is second.** Nothing else
   competes with them in the moment after Read item. Because the volunteer is holding the can
   and needs one answer, then one reason.
4. **Cobalt appears once per screen: the Read item button** (and its focus ring). Because it is
   the colour of the gloves, the hands doing the work; it marks the volunteer's own action and
   nothing else. Links are ink with an underline.
5. **A decision floods the answer panel with its colour, and colour is never text.** Keep green,
   set-aside yellow and discard red fill the whole answer panel (and the log chip), behind ink or
   white words, and every verdict also carries its word. Turn it and Nothing in view are
   instructions, not decisions, and stay on carton.
   Because colour alone fails for colour-blind volunteers and in a compressed video.
6. **Discard red appears only on a discard that two reads agreed on.** Because a wrong discard is
   the one mistake this app exists to prevent.
7. **The re-printed stamp is the signature device.** The waiting state shows a sample stamp;
   every verdict re-prints the one it read. The can lid appears only as the favicon and app icon
   (rounds 2 and 3: rings on the page read as radar decoration and crossed the stamp).
   Because the stamp is the thing being read.
8. **Illustrations appear only while waiting** (the empty answer panel, the empty shift log),
   never beside a decision. **They are two-ink halftone dots, black and cobalt, on nothing.** Carton white shows
   through only where the paper would (a white can, a white box). Cobalt is only ever the gloves.
   No disc, panel or shadow behind them. Because they are the coder's dots making a picture; a
   backing shape would turn them into clip art.
9. **Icons are dot-matrix pictograms on a 7 by 7 grid**, drawn in code. Never a stock icon set.
   Because the screen's only visual language is the coder's dot.
10. **Carton white is the only panel colour, and panels hold printed things** (the stamp, the
    item log). The canvas is lid grey. Because in the warehouse, print lands on cartons and lids.
11. **No viewfinder corner brackets, no scan line, no frosted glass.** Because that is every
    scanner app, and Keep Until reads dates rather than barcodes.
12. **The read-by badge is always next to the verdict** ("Read on this laptop" or "Read in the
    cloud", with the time). Because where the work happened is part of the answer in a hybrid app,
    and the judges are scoring exactly that.
13. **Titles in sentence case, no full stop, no trailing comma.** A question keeps its mark.
14. **Internal words never reach the screen**: mode names, route strings, JSON, "model",
    "inference", "triage". Because the reader is a volunteer, not the developer.
15. **One orchestrated moment: the stamp prints.** When a verdict arrives, the re-printed date
    appears column by column, about 300 ms, stepped like an inkjet head. Nothing moves on exit;
    with reduced motion it appears at once. On Android (2026-11-27) the moment has its lead-in and
    its run-out, all the coder's: while a read is out, seven dots step along Read item like the
    head crossing a lid and the last answer dims; on arrival the verdict's colour wipes across the
    panel from the left (never a blend: yellow into red passed through a muddy orange), the word
    sets, the date prints, then the reason and badge fade up. New log rows slide in; the tallies
    count. Nothing else moves.
18. **The answer is also in the hand.** On a phone each verdict has its own pulse: one firm pulse
    for keep, two light ticks for set aside, a long buzz for discard, a tick for turn it. Because the
    volunteer is often looking at the item, not the screen.
19. **Read item lives under the thumb.** On a phone the actions are pinned to the bottom edge and
    the answer scrolls above them. Because a volunteer with an item in each hand cannot scroll back
    up for every item.
16. **Developer controls live in a drawer**, closed by default (batch, export, inference mode,
    comparison, experiment). Because a volunteer should see one button.
17. **Never the word "expired" for a best-before date.** Only infant formula and meal
    replacements expire. Because calling best-before food expired is how good food gets binned.
