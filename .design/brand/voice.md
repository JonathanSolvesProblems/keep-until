# Voice

Who speaks: the sort lead standing beside a volunteer on their first shift. Plain, kind, sure.
Says what to do with the item in their hand, then why, in that order. Never explains the
machine.

## Five sample lines

- First screen: "Hold an item up to the camera"
- Button: "Read item"
- Empty state: "Nothing sorted yet. The first item you read shows up here and in the food bank's sheet."
- Error: "No Wi-Fi. Keep sorting: items save on this laptop and go to the sheet when it comes back."
- Confirmation: "Added to the sheet"

## Verdict lines (the words on the one screen that matters)

- KEEP: "Keep until 14 March 2027" then the reason: "Canned vegetables keep 12 months past best before. Food Banks Canada chart, page 4."
- DISCARD: "Discard" then "Boxed cereal keeps 6 months past best before, and this one is 9 months past. Two reads agreed on the date."
- SET ASIDE: "Set it aside for the lead" then the one-line reason ("It needs the fridge", "The two reads disagree on the date").
- TURN IT: "Turn it so the date faces the camera"
- NO ITEM: "Nothing in view"

## Banned phrases (into charter.json)

1. "expired" for a best-before date. Food Banks Canada's own guidance says people throw out good food because they call a best-before date expired. Use "past its best before". Only infant formula and meal replacements have an expiry date, and those say "past its expiry date".
2. "AI-powered"
3. "smart" (as in smart sorting)
4. "scan" for what the app does. A volunteer reads a can; the app reads the date.
5. "seamless"

## Vocabulary

Use: item, can, box, carton, best before, keep until, set aside, the lead, the sheet, the
chart, read, turn it, on this laptop, in the cloud.

Never on screen: inference, model, mode names (prefer_on_device), triage, confidence, JSON,
route strings, latency in milliseconds outside the read-by badge.

## Titles

Sentence case, no full stop, no comma at the end. A question keeps its question mark.
